How the petrol price cycle works

Prices don't move randomly. In these five cities they follow a pattern, and the bottom is the time to fill up.

  1. 1
    Prices climbRetailers push prices up over a few days. Rises of 20 cents a litre or more are common.
  2. 2
    Then they slowly fallOver the next couple of weeks, prices drift back down as stations compete.
  3. 3
    At the bottom, we email youWhen the ACCC says prices are around their lowest point, you get one email: fill up now, before prices climb again.

Typical cycle length, according to the ACCC: Sydney about 5 weeks · Melbourne about 6 weeks · Brisbane about 6½ weeks · Adelaide about 2½ weeks · Perth about 1 week.

FAQ

Is it really free?

Yes. FillYaTank is a free, non-commercial project run by one person. There are no ads and no paid plans, and your email is never sold or shared.

How often will you email me?

Only when your city reaches the bottom of its price cycle, roughly once per cycle: every few weeks in the eastern cities and about weekly in Perth. Nothing else.

Why is there a price cycle at all?

According to the ACCC, price cycles come from retailers' own pricing decisions, not from changes in wholesale costs. One or more retailers lead a sharp price rise, others follow, and prices then slowly drift back down. It isn't illegal, but there's no cost reason for it. If you think prices shouldn't work this way, tell your local MP. You can find your federal MP here.

Doesn't PetrolSpy already do this?

Sort of. Apps like PetrolSpy are great for finding the cheapest station near you, but you have to remember to check them, and they're supported by ads. FillYaTank comes to you instead: one email at the right time, no app and no ads.

Where does the information come from?

The ACCC (Australian Competition and Consumer Commission) publishes petrol buying tips for these five cities on weekdays. We check them every Monday, Wednesday and Friday. FillYaTank isn't affiliated with the ACCC.

How long do I have to fill up?

Aim to fill up the same day. In Perth, WA's FuelWatch rules lock each day's prices in for 24 hours from 6am. In the other cities prices can jump quickly once a new cycle starts, so sooner is better.

Why only these five cities?

Regular price cycles happen in Australia's five largest cities. The ACCC doesn't publish buying tips for Canberra, Hobart, Darwin or most regional areas because prices there don't cycle in the same way.

What do you store about me?

Just your email address and city. No names, no tracking cookies and no analytics scripts. Page visits are counted anonymously.

How do I unsubscribe?

Every email has a one-click unsubscribe link.
